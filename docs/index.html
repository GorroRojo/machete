It isn't working from a list.

There are no trigger words. Claude reads the whole conversation and your sources and keeps asking one thing: what would help most right now that isn't in front of you? A few cards that question produces:

  • The open item nobody raisedLast call, Alex promised the revised budget by Friday. Not mentioned yet.
  • A number that doesn't matchSomeone says about 200 customers. Your CRM says 164.
  • "That deck from October"The exact file, linked.
  • You were just asked somethingYou missed it. The question, in one line.
  • "What have we agreed so far?"The decisions made in this call, from its transcript.
  • Someone who isn't on the callMaria is out until the 28th, from her calendar.
  • Deadline mathSix weeks from Monday, skipping the holiday week: Mon 11 Jan.
  • Another time zone3 pm Eastern is 5:00 pm yours.
  • Money at today's rateThe converted amount, with the source of the rate.
  • A past decision that contradicts this oneOn 3 Nov you agreed on annual billing. The notes, linked.

The rules it keeps.

Something that follows your meetings has to be boring about limits. These are built in.

  • Nothing acts without your tap

    A card is a suggestion. No event, task or reminder exists until you tap ✓. Emails and chat messages are only ever drafts, and it never invites anyone.

  • No answer beats a wrong one

    Not found, or not sure? No card. The question goes on the end-of-meeting list, and so does anything that needs more than one or two quick lookups.

  • Rare on purpose

    No suggestions in the first two minutes, then one card every two minutes at most, about six an hour, never the same thing twice. Only the answer to a question asked out loud can come at any time.

  • Your meeting isn't passed around

    Machete's files stay on your computer. The transcript goes to Claude, which reads it to decide on cards, and nowhere else. Your tools receive only the one action you tapped. Web lookups are on by default for public facts; one line in the settings turns them off.

  • Off for private calls

    If the calendar event is marked private or mentions a doctor, therapy or any word you list, it switches itself off before reading a line. This needs your calendar connected; without it, you turn the copilot off yourself.

  • Hidden from screen shares

    By default the window doesn't show when you share your screen (Windows 10 version 2004 or newer). The first card of every meeting reminds you to tell people you're recording.

How it works.

  1. You record the call the way you already do. No bot joins. It reads Wispr Flow's live meeting transcript, a transcript file another tool writes during the call, or your microphone and computer sound with Whisper running locally.
  2. A small background listener notices the meeting start, opens the cards window and starts a Claude Code session.
  3. Claude reads along a few seconds behind and looks things up through the connectors your Claude already has.
  4. A card appears. ✓ does exactly what it says. ✕ dismisses it.

A notetaker gives you the record after the call. Machete is for the moment during it. Keep both.

Which recorders work?

Wispr Flow's meeting recording is what it runs on today. It also reads a transcript file that another tool writes while the meeting runs, or your microphone and computer sound with local Whisper, which runs further behind because it transcribes in 8-second pieces. Granola, Otter and Fireflies are not supported, because they don't write a live transcript file on your computer. Zoom or Meet captions only work if a tool saves them to a file as the meeting goes.

What can it look in and act on?

Whatever your Claude is connected to: Google or Outlook calendar, Gmail or Outlook drafts, Slack drafts, ClickUp, Asana, Notion, Linear, Google Sheets, a CRM, plus a notes folder on your computer. With no task tool, a ✓ on a task sets a reminder on your computer.

Where does my transcript go?

To Claude, like anything else in a Claude Code session, and nowhere else. Your recorder is a separate matter: Wispr Flow makes its transcript with its own service, while local Whisper transcribes on your machine.

What does it cost?

Machete is free (MIT). You bring your own Claude subscription, and your recorder if it's a paid one.

Does it work on a Mac?

Nobody has tried yet. The code is written to run there, but it has only been tested on Windows, so expect to fix a few things.

One short prompt sets it up.

  1. Open Claude Code and paste the prompt.
  2. Answer five short questions: your recorder, your calendar, your task tool, where your notes live, your language.
  3. Watch the test. It runs a made-up meeting and shows you your first card.
  4. Record your next meeting. The cards window opens by itself.

You need: Claude Code with a Claude subscription, Python 3.11 or newer, and something that records your meetings. Git helps; without it, Claude Code downloads the zip.
